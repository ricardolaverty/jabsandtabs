Nausea is the most common side effect of Mounjaro (tirzepatide), Wegovy (semaglutide) and other GLP-1 medicines. In clinical trials it affected roughly a quarter to a third of people on tirzepatide and over four in ten on semaglutide 2.4 mg, mostly mildly or moderately and mainly during the first weeks and after dose increases. For most people it eases with time. Small, low-fat meals, eating slowly, stopping at the first sign of fullness and steady sipping of fluids help many people. If nausea is severe, persistent or stops you eating and drinking, your prescriber can review your dose.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Nausea is very common on GLP-1 medicines and is listed as very common in the Mounjaro and Wegovy product information.
- It is usually mild to moderate, and is most frequent when starting treatment and after each dose increase.
- It happens mainly because these medicines slow stomach emptying and act on areas of the brain involved in appetite and nausea.
- Practical measures, such as smaller meals, less fat, eating slowly and stopping when full, help many people.
- Your prescriber may slow the pace of dose increases or step back a dose if nausea is troublesome. Do not change doses yourself.
- Seek urgent help for severe, persistent abdominal pain, vomiting that stops you keeping fluids down, or signs of dehydration.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Why GLP-1 medicines cause nausea

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is a hormone the gut releases after eating. Medicines such as semaglutide mimic it; tirzepatide also acts on a second hormone receptor, GIP. These medicines help with weight loss partly by:

- **slowing stomach emptying**, so food stays in the stomach for longer and you feel full sooner
- **acting on the brain**, including areas involved in appetite, satiety and nausea

Both effects can cause nausea, especially when your body is adjusting to a new dose. If you eat more than your stomach can comfortably handle at its new slower pace, or eat foods that are slow to digest, such as fatty or fried foods, the feeling of fullness can tip into nausea, and sometimes vomiting.

## How common is nausea?

### Mounjaro (tirzepatide)

In the SURMOUNT-1 trial of tirzepatide for weight management, published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2022, nausea was the most common adverse event:

| Group | Proportion reporting nausea |
|---|---|
| Tirzepatide 5 mg | about 25% |
| Tirzepatide 10 mg | about 33% |
| Tirzepatide 15 mg | about 31% |
| Placebo | about 10% |

Most cases were mild to moderate and occurred mainly during the dose-escalation period. The Mounjaro product information lists nausea as very common (affecting more than one in ten people).

### Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4 mg)

The Wegovy product information, based on pooled trial data, reports nausea in about 44% of people on semaglutide 2.4 mg. In the STEP 1 trial, nausea was also the most common side effect, typically mild to moderate and most frequent during the 16-week dose-escalation period.

### Oral GLP-1 medicines

Tablet forms of GLP-1 medicines also commonly cause nausea. The details differ by product, so check the patient information leaflet. Our guide to [oral GLP-1 side effects](/guides/oral-glp1-side-effects) covers these.

## How long does it last?

The pattern most people experience is:

- **First few weeks:** nausea is most likely when starting the lowest dose, as your body adjusts.
- **After dose increases:** nausea often returns for a short time after each step up, then settles.
- **Weekly pattern:** some people notice nausea is strongest in the first day or two after their weekly injection, easing towards the end of the week.
- **Longer term:** for most people, nausea becomes less frequent and less intense once they are established on a maintenance dose.

There is wide variation. Some people never experience nausea; a minority find it persistent. If nausea continues for weeks at the same dose, or worsens, speak to your prescriber.

<Callout type="info">
Dose increases on GLP-1 medicines are deliberately gradual to help reduce side effects. Mounjaro usually starts at 2.5 mg weekly and increases in 2.5 mg steps no more often than every four weeks. Your prescriber can keep you on a dose for longer if you are struggling. See our [Mounjaro dosing schedule](/guides/mounjaro-dosing-schedule) and [Wegovy dosing schedule](/guides/wegovy-dosing-schedule) guides.
</Callout>

## Practical ways to manage nausea

### Eating habits

These are the strategies most consistently recommended by clinicians and in the 2025 joint advisory on nutritional priorities for GLP-1 therapy:

- **Eat smaller meals, more often.** The advisory described a small breakfast followed by small meals every three to four hours.
- **Stop at the first sign of fullness.** On GLP-1 medicines this signal can arrive suddenly. Continuing to eat is a common trigger for nausea and vomiting.
- **Eat slowly.** Put your cutlery down between mouthfuls and take your time.
- **Choose lower-fat foods.** Fatty, fried and rich foods slow digestion further. The advisory suggested avoiding fatty foods, particularly early on.
- **Avoid very large meals**, especially in the evening.
- **Do not lie down straight after eating.** Stay upright for two to three hours.

### What to eat when you feel sick

Many people find plain, bland, or cold foods easier when they feel queasy. Options to try include:

| Often easier | Often harder |
|---|---|
| Dry toast, crackers, rice cakes | Fried and greasy foods |
| Plain rice, pasta or potatoes | Creamy or cheesy sauces |
| Clear soups and broths | Large plated meals |
| Bananas, apples, pears | Very sweet foods and drinks |
| Plain or Greek-style yoghurt | Strong-smelling foods |
| Cold foods such as cold chicken, salads, sandwiches | Very spicy foods |
| Ginger biscuits or ginger tea | Alcohol |
| Ice lollies, ice chips | Fizzy drinks (for some people) |

Protein remains important for preserving muscle during weight loss, so try to include some when you can, perhaps a boiled egg, some yoghurt or a little cold chicken. Our guide to [protein intake on weight loss injections](/guides/protein-on-glp1) has more ideas, and our guide to [foods that can worsen GLP-1 side effects](/guides/foods-that-worsen-glp1-side-effects) covers common triggers in detail.

### Drinking

- **Sip fluids steadily through the day** rather than drinking large amounts at once.
- **Drink between meals** rather than with them, to avoid overfilling the stomach.
- **Try cold drinks** or ice chips if warm drinks make you feel worse, or vice versa.
- **Ginger or peppermint tea** may help some people.

Dehydration can make nausea worse, creating a cycle. See our guide to [hydration on GLP-1 medication](/guides/hydration-on-glp1).

### Other self-help measures

The NHS suggests several general measures for nausea that may also help on GLP-1 treatment:

- getting plenty of fresh air
- distracting yourself, for example by listening to music or watching something
- avoiding strong smells and cooking smells where possible
- eating foods containing ginger

### Ginger

Ginger has been studied for nausea in other settings, such as pregnancy, with some evidence of benefit. It has not been specifically studied for GLP-1-related nausea. Ginger tea, ginger biscuits or crystallised ginger are low-risk options for most people. If you take blood-thinning medicines, check with a pharmacist before using concentrated ginger supplements.

### Timing your injection

Mounjaro can be injected at any time of day, with or without meals, according to the product information, as long as it is on the same day each week. Some people prefer to inject in the evening so that the first hours of any nausea happen while they sleep. Others choose a day that means the more difficult first day or two fall at the weekend. If you want to change your injection day, check the rules in your patient information leaflet and with your prescriber.

## Planning your week around nausea

Because GLP-1 injections are given once a week, many people notice a rhythm to their symptoms. Planning around that rhythm can make nausea easier to live with. The pattern below is an example, not a rule; your own experience may be quite different.

| Time since injection | What some people notice | Ideas that may help |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 to 2 | Fullness and nausea most noticeable, appetite lowest | Small, plain meals; soups, eggs, yoghurt, toast; sip fluids; avoid big social meals if possible |
| Day 3 to 4 | Nausea easing, appetite still reduced | Gradually return to balanced small meals with protein and vegetables |
| Day 5 to 7 | Appetite may return somewhat | Keep portions modest; this can be a good time for more substantial meals or eating out |
| After a dose increase | Symptoms may return or be stronger for one to two weeks | Go back to the "day 1 to 2" approach until things settle; tell your prescriber if they do not |

### Keep a simple symptom diary

For two or three weeks, jot down the day and time of your injection, what and how much you ate, when nausea occurred and how bad it was on a scale of 0 to 10, and anything that helped. This can reveal patterns, such as particular foods, large evening meals or eating too fast, and it gives your prescriber useful information if you need to discuss your dose.

### Prepare for difficult days

It helps to have a "nausea kit" ready before the days you expect to feel worse:

- plain crackers, rice cakes or dry toast
- ginger tea bags or ginger biscuits
- small pots of yoghurt
- ready-made soup portions in the freezer
- ice lollies or frozen fruit
- a refillable water bottle
- oral rehydration sachets from a pharmacy, in case of vomiting

## Common myths about GLP-1 nausea

### "You just have to push through it"

Mild, short-lived nausea is common and often settles. But persistent or severe nausea is not something you are expected to endure. Your prescriber can adjust the pace of your treatment. Pushing through vomiting and dehydration can be harmful, including for your kidneys.

### "Eating more will settle your stomach"

On an empty stomach, a small amount of plain food can sometimes help. But eating larger amounts to try to settle nausea usually makes things worse on GLP-1 medicines, because the stomach empties more slowly. Little and often is the safer approach.

### "Nausea means the dose is too high for you"

Not necessarily. Nausea after a dose increase is very common and often temporary. Whether a dose is right for you is a decision for your prescriber, taking into account how you are tolerating it, your progress and your overall health.

### "If you don't feel sick, the medicine isn't working"

As explained above, nausea and effectiveness are not linked in a simple way. Many people do well with few side effects.

## Medical options

### Reviewing your dose

If nausea is persistent or affects your ability to eat and drink, the most important step is to speak to your prescriber. Depending on your situation, they may:

- keep you on your current dose for longer before increasing
- return you temporarily to a lower dose
- review your other medicines
- consider whether another treatment would suit you better

These are prescribing decisions that only your prescriber can make. Do not skip doses, split doses or change your dose without advice.

### Anti-sickness medicines

In some cases, a prescriber may consider a short course of an anti-sickness medicine. These have their own side effects and interactions, so they should only be used on professional advice. A pharmacist can also advise on whether any over-the-counter options are suitable for you.

### Constipation as a cause of nausea

Constipation, which is also very common on GLP-1 medicines, can itself cause nausea, bloating and reduced appetite. If you have not opened your bowels for several days and feel sick, treating the constipation may help. See our guide to [constipation on GLP-1 medication](/guides/constipation-on-glp1).

### Reflux and indigestion

Indigestion, reflux and burping are listed as common side effects in the Mounjaro product information and can feel similar to, or accompany, nausea. Our guide to [reflux, burping and indigestion on GLP-1 medication](/guides/reflux-burping-and-indigestion-on-glp1) covers this.

## Nausea and other medicines

### Oral contraceptives

Vomiting within a few hours of taking an oral contraceptive pill can reduce its effectiveness. In addition, the Mounjaro product information advises switching to a non-oral contraceptive method, or adding a barrier method, for four weeks after starting treatment and for four weeks after each dose increase. See our guide to [GLP-1 medicines and contraception](/guides/glp1-and-contraception).

### Other oral medicines

GLP-1 medicines slow stomach emptying, which may affect how quickly some oral medicines are absorbed. Vomiting can also mean medicines are not absorbed. If you take medicines where precise levels are important, such as some epilepsy medicines, warfarin or thyroid medicines, and you are vomiting, speak to your GP or pharmacist. Our guide to [GLP-1 medication interactions](/guides/glp1-medication-interactions) has more detail.

### Diabetes medicines

If you have diabetes and take insulin or a sulphonylurea, eating less because of nausea increases the risk of low blood sugar. Follow your diabetes team's advice, including any "sick day rules".

### Surgery and anaesthesia

Because GLP-1 medicines slow stomach emptying, there may be a higher risk of stomach contents being inhaled during a general anaesthetic or sedation. Always tell the team if you are having a procedure. See our guide to [GLP-1 medicines, surgery and anaesthesia](/guides/glp1-surgery-and-anaesthesia).

## When nausea might be something else

Most nausea on GLP-1 treatment is a manageable side effect. However, nausea and vomiting can occasionally be signs of something more serious. The product information for these medicines lists:

- **acute pancreatitis** (rare for Mounjaro): severe, persistent abdominal pain, often spreading to the back, with or without vomiting
- **gallbladder problems**, including gallstones (common in the weight-management setting for Mounjaro): pain in the upper right abdomen, fever, yellowing of the skin or eyes
- **dehydration and kidney problems**: from persistent vomiting or diarrhoea

Nausea can also, of course, be caused by things unrelated to your medicine, such as infections, other illnesses or pregnancy. GLP-1 medicines for weight loss should not be used in pregnancy. If you could be pregnant, take a test and speak to your prescriber.

## When to speak to a professional

Contact your prescriber, GP or pharmacist if:

- nausea lasts more than a week or two at the same dose, or is getting worse
- nausea stops you eating enough or affects your daily life
- you are vomiting regularly, even if you can keep some fluids down
- you are losing weight much faster than expected
- you have diabetes and are having low blood sugar readings
- you take an oral contraceptive pill or other important medicines and have been vomiting
- you think you could be pregnant

Seek urgent medical help (call NHS 111, or 999 in an emergency) if you have:

- severe abdominal pain that does not go away, particularly if it spreads to your back, with or without vomiting (possible pancreatitis)
- pain in the upper right of your tummy, a high temperature, or yellowing of the skin or the whites of your eyes (possible gallbladder problems)
- vomiting that means you cannot keep down any fluids for more than a few hours
- signs of dehydration, such as passing little or no urine, dizziness, confusion or a fast heartbeat
- vomit that contains blood or looks like coffee grounds
- a swollen, hard abdomen with no bowel movements or wind, especially with vomiting

Decisions about your dose, pausing or stopping treatment belong to your prescriber.
