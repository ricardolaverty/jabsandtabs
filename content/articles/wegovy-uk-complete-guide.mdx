Wegovy is a prescription-only weight-loss medicine containing semaglutide, made by Novo Nordisk. In the UK it is licensed for adults with obesity, or overweight plus a weight-related health condition, alongside a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity, and to reduce the risk of heart attack and stroke in certain adults. It is most often taken as a once-weekly injection, building up to a 2.4mg maintenance dose, with a higher 7.2mg dose approved by the MHRA in January 2026. A once-daily Wegovy tablet was approved in June 2026. This guide covers how Wegovy works, who can be prescribed it, what the trials show, side effects, NHS access and what affects cost.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Wegovy contains semaglutide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist that reduces appetite and food intake.
- It is licensed in the UK for weight management in adults with a BMI of 30 or more, or 27 or more with a weight-related condition, and in some adolescents aged 12 and over.
- In STEP 1, adults without diabetes lost about 15% of body weight on average over 68 weeks on 2.4mg, versus about 2% on placebo.
- The SELECT trial found a 20% relative reduction in major cardiovascular events in people with established heart disease and overweight or obesity.
- The weekly dose increases gradually from 0.25mg to 2.4mg over 16 weeks; a 7.2mg dose is now licensed for some adults with obesity.
- On the NHS, NICE TA875 limits Wegovy to specialist weight management services for up to two years. Private treatment requires assessment by a regulated prescriber.
</KeyTakeaways>

## What is Wegovy?

### The basics

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Active ingredient | Semaglutide |
| Manufacturer | Novo Nordisk |
| Medicine class | GLP-1 receptor agonist |
| Form | Weekly injection (FlexTouch pen); once-daily tablet also approved |
| Injection doses | 0.25mg, 0.5mg, 1mg, 1.7mg, 2.4mg, and 7.2mg for some adults |
| UK weight management licence | MHRA authorised in September 2021 |
| NICE guidance | TA875 (March 2023) |
| Status | Prescription-only medicine |

Semaglutide is a modified version of the natural hormone GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1). Natural GLP-1 lasts only minutes in the bloodstream, but semaglutide has been engineered to last around a week, allowing once-weekly injection.

The same active ingredient is found in Ozempic, a weekly injection for type 2 diabetes, and Rybelsus, a daily tablet for type 2 diabetes. Wegovy is the semaglutide product licensed specifically for weight management. Our guides to [Ozempic vs Wegovy](/guides/ozempic-vs-wegovy) and [semaglutide in the UK](/guides/semaglutide-uk-guide) explain the differences. For an overview of the medicine, see the [Wegovy hub](/wegovy).

### Licensed uses in the UK

According to the UK product information, Wegovy is licensed:

- **For weight management in adults**, alongside a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity, with a BMI of 30 kg/m² or more, or 27 to under 30 kg/m² with at least one weight-related condition.
- **For weight management in adolescents aged 12 and over** with obesity, under appropriate specialist care.
- **To reduce cardiovascular risk** in adults with established cardiovascular disease and a BMI of 27 kg/m² or more, based on the SELECT trial.

The UK leaflet also lists use in the liver condition metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) in adults. Licensed indications can change; always check the current Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) on the Electronic Medicines Compendium.

## How Wegovy works

Semaglutide acts on GLP-1 receptors in several parts of the body:

- **The brain.** It acts on areas that regulate hunger, fullness and food reward, reducing appetite and cravings.
- **The stomach.** It slows stomach emptying, which helps people feel full for longer, especially early in treatment.
- **The pancreas.** It increases insulin release and reduces glucagon when blood sugar is raised, which is why it also improves blood sugar control.

The result is that people tend to eat less without feeling as hungry, which leads to weight loss over time. Our detailed guide to [how Wegovy works](/guides/how-does-wegovy-work) covers the science.

## Doses and how it is taken

### The injection schedule

Wegovy injections are taken once a week, on the same day each week, at any time of day, with or without food. Treatment starts at a low dose and increases every four weeks to reduce gastrointestinal side effects.

| Weeks | Weekly dose |
|---|---|
| 1 to 4 | 0.25mg |
| 5 to 8 | 0.5mg |
| 9 to 12 | 1mg |
| 13 to 16 | 1.7mg |
| 17 onwards | 2.4mg (maintenance) |
| From week 21, if appropriate | 7.2mg (adults with obesity only) |

Source: UK Wegovy Patient Information Leaflet. The UK SmPC states 2.4mg as the maintenance dose. If gastrointestinal side effects are significant, it allows the prescriber to delay escalation or lower to the previous dose until symptoms improve. The SmPC also says that if at least 5% of initial body weight has not been lost after six months, a decision is needed on whether to continue.

### The 7.2mg dose

In January 2026, the MHRA approved a 7.2mg weekly dose for adults with obesity (a BMI of 30 or more), making the UK the first country to do so. Approval was based on the STEP UP trial, in which adults with obesity taking 7.2mg lost on average about 19% of body weight over 72 weeks, compared with about 4% on placebo.

According to the UK leaflet, 7.2mg can be considered after at least four weeks on 2.4mg, and was initially given as three 2.4mg injections on the same day. A single-injection 7.2mg pen has since been reported as available in the UK; check the current SmPC and speak to your prescriber about how it is supplied. The leaflet notes that some side effects, including hair loss and altered skin sensations, are reported more often at 7.2mg. NICE had not assessed the 7.2mg dose for NHS use at the time of writing.

### The pen

In the UK, Wegovy injections are supplied as FlexTouch pens, each containing four weekly doses of a single strength. A new needle is attached for each injection. Our guide to [using the Wegovy FlexTouch pen](/guides/wegovy-pen-how-to-inject) explains the steps, and [Wegovy doses explained](/guides/wegovy-dosing-schedule) covers titration, missed doses and dose changes in more detail.

### Wegovy tablets

The MHRA approved oral semaglutide for weight management, marketed as Wegovy tablets, in June 2026. In the OASIS 4 trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2025, adults taking a 25mg daily tablet lost on average about 14% of body weight, similar to the injection. The tablet must be taken on an empty stomach with a small sip of water, with no food or drink for at least 30 minutes afterwards. NHS access requires a NICE recommendation, which had not been made at the time of writing. See our [oral semaglutide guide](/guides/oral-semaglutide-guide) and the [oral GLP-1 hub](/oral-glp1).

## What the trials show

Wegovy was studied in the STEP (Semaglutide Treatment Effect in People with obesity) programme. Key trials include:

| Trial | Population | Length | Average weight change |
|---|---|---|---|
| STEP 1 | Adults with obesity/overweight, no diabetes | 68 weeks | About -15% vs -2% on placebo |
| STEP 2 | Adults with obesity/overweight and type 2 diabetes | 68 weeks | About -10% vs -3% on placebo |
| STEP 3 | With intensive behavioural therapy | 68 weeks | About -16% vs -6% on placebo |
| STEP 4 | After 20 weeks on semaglutide, randomised to continue or switch | 48 weeks after run-in | Continued: further -8%; switched to placebo: regained about 7% |
| STEP 5 | Adults with obesity/overweight, no diabetes | 104 weeks | About -15% vs -3% on placebo |
| STEP UP | Adults with obesity; 7.2mg vs 2.4mg vs placebo | 72 weeks | About -19% on 7.2mg vs -4% on placebo |

### Results in context

In STEP 1, about 86% of people on semaglutide lost at least 5% of their body weight, about 69% lost at least 10%, and about half lost at least 15%. Some people lost much less, and around one in seven did not reach 5%. Our guide to [Wegovy results](/guides/wegovy-results-timeline) explains the timeline and what affects individual response.

### Heart health: the SELECT trial

SELECT, published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2023, enrolled more than 17,000 adults with established cardiovascular disease and a BMI of 27 or more, without diabetes. Over an average follow-up of more than three years, semaglutide 2.4mg reduced the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (cardiovascular death, non-fatal heart attack or non-fatal stroke) by 20% compared with placebo. In absolute terms, these events occurred in about 6.5% of people on semaglutide and 8.0% on placebo. See [Wegovy and heart health](/guides/wegovy-heart-health-select-trial).

### Compared with tirzepatide

In SURMOUNT-5, the first head-to-head trial in obesity, tirzepatide (Mounjaro) produced greater average weight loss than semaglutide over 72 weeks: about 20% versus about 14%. That trial used semaglutide up to 2.4mg, not the newer 7.2mg dose. See [Mounjaro vs Wegovy](/mounjaro-vs-wegovy) and [SURMOUNT-5 explained](/guides/surmount-5-tirzepatide-vs-semaglutide).

## Side effects

### Common side effects

The most common side effects are gastrointestinal: nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain, indigestion, bloating and burping. These are most likely when starting treatment and after each dose increase, and usually improve over time. In STEP 1, about 4.5% of people on semaglutide stopped treatment because of gastrointestinal side effects, compared with under 1% on placebo.

Other common side effects listed in the UK leaflet include headache, tiredness, dizziness, hair loss, and, in people with diabetes, low blood sugar.

### Less common but serious

- **Gallbladder problems**, including gallstones.
- **Acute pancreatitis.**
- **Dehydration and kidney problems**, usually after significant vomiting or diarrhoea.
- **Diabetic retinopathy complications** in people with type 2 diabetes.
- **Aspiration during anaesthesia**, because of delayed stomach emptying.
- **Allergic reactions.**

Semaglutide should not be used in pregnancy, and people who could become pregnant should use effective contraception. Our [Wegovy side effects guide](/guides/wegovy-side-effects-guide) covers what to expect and how side effects are managed.

## Who should not take Wegovy

Wegovy may not be suitable if you:

- Are pregnant, planning pregnancy or breastfeeding.
- Have had an allergic reaction to semaglutide or any ingredient.
- Have a history of pancreatitis.
- Have severe gastrointestinal disease, such as gastroparesis.
- Have an active or past eating disorder.
- Have severe kidney or liver problems.

Many prescribers also ask about a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2. This reflects thyroid tumours seen in rodent studies and a warning in US labelling; the relevance to humans is uncertain. This is not a complete list, and in the UK the only absolute contraindication in the SmPC is hypersensitivity, so many of these are matters of caution and clinical judgement. A prescriber will review your full medical history. See [who shouldn't take GLP-1 medications](/guides/who-should-not-take-glp1s).

## Getting Wegovy in the UK

### On the NHS

NICE technology appraisal TA875, published in March 2023, recommends semaglutide for weight management on the NHS only within specialist weight management services, for adults with:

- A BMI of at least 35 kg/m² and at least one weight-related condition, or
- A BMI of 30 to 34.9 kg/m² who meet the criteria for referral to specialist weight management services.

Lower BMI thresholds, usually reduced by 2.5 kg/m², apply to people from certain ethnic backgrounds. NHS treatment is limited to a maximum of two years. Access to specialist services varies widely across the UK. See [Wegovy on the NHS](/guides/wegovy-on-the-nhs) and [private vs NHS weight loss treatment](/guides/private-vs-nhs-weight-loss-treatment).

### Privately

Many people in the UK receive Wegovy through private prescribers, including online pharmacies and clinics regulated by the General Pharmaceutical Council (GPhC) or the Care Quality Commission (CQC). A legitimate service will:

- Carry out a medical consultation, including your BMI, medical history and current medicines.
- Verify your identity and, typically, your weight.
- Only prescribe if treatment is clinically appropriate and within the licence.
- Offer follow-up and a way to report side effects.

Our guides to [how to choose an online pharmacy](/guides/how-to-choose-online-pharmacy-weight-loss) and [why online pharmacies verify your ID and weight](/guides/id-and-weight-verification) explain what to look for.

## Common myths about Wegovy

**"Wegovy works without any change in diet."** Wegovy reduces appetite, which makes eating less easier, but it is licensed for use alongside a reduced-calorie diet and physical activity. In the trials, everyone received lifestyle advice.

**"Everyone loses 15%."** That is an average. Some people lose much more, and some lose little. Response varies with factors such as diabetes status, dose tolerated and adherence.

**"You can stop once you hit your target and keep the weight off."** The STEP 1 extension and STEP 4 trials showed that most people regain weight after stopping. Any plan to stop should be made with your prescriber.

**"Ozempic and Wegovy are interchangeable."** They contain the same active ingredient, but are different products with different licences, pens and doses. Our [Ozempic vs Wegovy guide](/guides/ozempic-vs-wegovy) explains why this matters.

## Questions to ask your prescriber

1. Am I eligible under the licence, and would I qualify for NHS treatment?
2. Is Wegovy or another option more suitable for my health conditions?
3. How should I manage my other medicines, particularly for diabetes or blood pressure?
4. What should I do if side effects make it hard to increase my dose?
5. How will my progress be reviewed, and what happens if I do not lose much weight?
6. What is the plan for the long term?

## Long-term use and stopping

Weight regain after stopping is common. In the STEP 1 extension, people who stopped semaglutide regained about two-thirds of the weight they had lost within a year. In STEP 4, people switched to placebo after 20 weeks regained weight, while those who continued lost more.

This reflects the view that obesity is a long-term condition. Decisions about how long to continue, whether to reduce the dose, or how to stop should be made with your prescriber. See [Wegovy maintenance and stopping](/guides/wegovy-maintenance-and-stopping) and [long-term GLP-1 use](/guides/long-term-glp1-use).

## Lifestyle alongside treatment

Wegovy is licensed for use alongside a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity, and the trials included lifestyle support. While appetite is reduced, it helps to focus on:

- **Protein** at each meal to help preserve muscle.
- **Fibre and fluids** to reduce constipation.
- **Smaller, slower meals** to reduce nausea.
- **Strength training** alongside general activity.

See [what to eat on GLP-1 medication](/guides/what-to-eat-on-glp1) and [preserving muscle on GLP-1 treatment](/guides/preserving-muscle-on-glp1).

## When to speak to a professional

Speak to your GP, pharmacist or a regulated prescriber if you are considering Wegovy, so that they can assess whether it is suitable and safe for you. Tell them about all your medical conditions and medicines, especially diabetes medicines such as insulin or sulfonylureas, and whether you are pregnant or planning pregnancy.

During treatment, contact your prescriber if:

- Side effects stop you eating or drinking normally.
- You are not losing weight as expected, or you are losing weight very quickly.
- You notice changes in your mood, or thoughts of self-harm.
- You have vision changes and you have diabetes.

Seek **urgent medical help** (NHS 111, or 999 / A&E in an emergency) if you have:

- **Severe, persistent abdominal pain**, which may spread to your back, with or without vomiting. This can be a sign of pancreatitis.
- **Pain in the upper right abdomen, fever or yellowing of the skin or eyes**, which can suggest gallbladder problems.
- **Signs of severe dehydration**, such as dizziness, confusion or passing very little urine.
- **Signs of a serious allergic reaction**, such as swelling of the face, lips or throat, or difficulty breathing.

Prescribing decisions, including dose changes and how long to continue, belong to your prescriber.
